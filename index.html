01 · Research foundation

From fragmented supervision to one transparent workflow

IPMS addresses the disconnected planning, monitoring, assessment, and administrative work surrounding undergraduate software projects.

The challenge

Supervisors see reports, meetings, repositories, and schedules, but rarely as one connected story.

Manual processes make it difficult to form balanced teams, identify proposal weaknesses, distribute work fairly, detect delivery risk early, and verify whether reported progress is supported by evidence.

01

Skill and preference conflicts during team formation

02

Subjective planning and uneven individual workload

03

Late risk detection and incomplete contribution visibility

04

Slow, inconsistent proposal feedback and mentorship

Research objective
◎

Build an intelligent, human-governed project management system for undergraduate research.

The platform combines multi-objective optimization, machine learning, NLP, scheduling algorithms, and auditable evidence while leaving academic decisions with authorized staff.

Four components. Four distinct evidence stories.

The project combines optimisation, planning, proposal assessment and risk monitoring. Each result below names its evaluation data and its limit; the figures should not be combined into one system accuracy.

01 / OPTIMISATIONExact + case study

Intelligent Team Formation

Heuristic-seeded NSGA-II explores feasible team and project allocations.

0.9479mean hypervolume, versus 0.6532 for unseeded NSGA-II (about 45.1% higher)
Exact verification
34,650 feasible allocations enumerated for 12 students
Real-response case
60 students; 100% modelled technical coverage and 95% preference satisfaction
More evaluation details
  • Medium- and high-conflict Pareto fronts were recovered in the exact verification.
  • Friedman chi-square(3) = 326.08, p < 0.001; Kendall W = 0.906.
  • The synthetic 1,000-student / 250-project pipeline ran in about 30.15 seconds.

Boundary: Competencies were self-reported; one cohort case does not establish institution-wide outcomes.

02 / PLANNINGHistorical + fixtures

Adaptive Scheduling

Proposal tasks, effort estimates and member assignments become reviewable plans.

73 / 75explicit tasks recovered from three controlled synthetic proposals (97.3%)
JIRA proxy classifier
81.27% held-out accuracy; weighted F1 0.815 on 1,411 issues
Workload balance
LPT CV 3.72% versus guarded hybrid 4.40% across 100 offline trials
More evaluation details
  • Effort-model log MAE = 0.8554 and log RMSE = 1.0614; error in original hours remains large.
  • The draft reports 6/6 focused scheduling runtime checks passed in an isolated PostgreSQL database.
  • Live assignment uses LPT; the hybrid comparison and JIRA classifier are offline research evidence.

Boundary: JIRA outcome labels are not student-assignment accuracy; fixture recovery is not general proposal accuracy.

03 / ASSESSMENTDataset pipeline

Proposal Quality & Mentorship

Annotated weaknesses and historical feedback support supervisor-led review.

2,151feedback records embedded for retrieval; this is a data-pipeline count, not model accuracy
Prepared annotations
2,121 deduplicated spans from 2,227 raw annotations
Grading evidence
165 records on a 0-42 scale; final selected-model performance unverified
More evaluation details
  • 2,226 raw historical-feedback records were prepared before 2,151 were embedded.
  • Selected MiniLM/Logistic Regression and Ridge figures in the draft are performance targets only.
  • The reported backend test suite stopped during collection because a required dependency was missing.

Boundary: The draft's 85% classification target and grading targets are not achieved results.

04 / MONITORINGSynthetic + prototype

Risk & Contribution Analytics

Repository and issue-tracker activity inform contribution and early-warning views.

97.63%Logistic Regression held-out accuracy on prepared synthetic risk labels
Experiment
4,000 synthetic records; 3,200 train / 800 test; 12 features
Prototype
GitHub/Jira retrieval, dashboard analytics and HIGH RISK email delivery demonstrated
More evaluation details
  • Logistic Regression weighted F1 was approximately 0.98 on the synthetic test set.
  • Comparator accuracy: Gradient Boosting 94.00%, Random Forest 92.38%, Decision Tree 90.25%.
  • External-API and alert demonstrations establish operation, not independently labelled real-project prediction.

Boundary: High accuracy on synthetic labels is not measured accuracy for real student-project risk.

Integration status

Team Formation's active-allocation API was consumed and displayed in Scheduling. Automatic backend group-ID mapping and direct Quality/Risk data exchange across all four services have not been verified.

Source & interpretation

Figures follow the R26-IT-095 draft report, Section 6.3. They come from different datasets and evaluation methods; no single aggregate IPMS accuracy or full-system usability result is claimed.
